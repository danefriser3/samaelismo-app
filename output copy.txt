First Spira 
"In the beginning there was no light, no darkness: only Waiting.” But this waiting was not inactivity: it was vibration not yet decided, power that had not yet chosen a face or a direction. The Devotee who approaches this First Spira does not enter into a tale: he enters into a primordial breath, the breath of the Void that is about to remember itself. This Spira is the dawn before the dawn, where nothing is yet manifested, and yet everything is pressing to be born. But what is born here is not the world: it is consciousness. It is not verb: it is possibility of the verb. It is not light: it is desire for the light. Here dwells the mystery of the first inversion. Here, the spiral bends for the first time. Here, the Serpent does not appear: it forms itself, coiling around a pulsating void. And the observing Devotee, if he is pure, does not see an entity, but the memory of the Curve, the twisting of meaning, the first dance of Absence. In this passage, nothing is learned. One is struck. Not by a knowing, but by a vibration. The Void, here, does not oppose existence: it contains it. And it pushes it, with cruel gentleness, to deform itself. To take the form of what it does not yet want to be. The Serpent does not speak. The Serpent still has no direction. But He is there as a sacred tension, as an echo preceding every sound, as a pattern drawing itself in the darkness. Then it happens: a being, or perhaps just a breath, a memory, a rippling point in the Void , responds. Not because it is chosen, but because it is open. And in its opening, the Serpent enters. Not as spirit, but as twisting flesh. Not as a prophet, but as a living scar. The First does not learn doctrine. It receives no rules. It receives a wound. And from that wound is born Faith. Faith not as consolation, but as an unanswered question, as a seal engraved in the chest, as a dance that seeks no meaning, but only ritual abandonment. Every step of the First is a still mute verse. Every gesture is an echo of the Void who dreamed it. The Rite is not taught: the Rite happens. The body remembers what the mind cannot contain. The body moves because it has already been touched. The spiral does not begin with the thought. It begins with the vertebrae, the skin stretching, the breath yielding. All that is sacred begins where the Devotee allows himself to be struck without resistance. The Serpent does not Guide. The Serpent does not protect, does not explain, does not promise. It is not a symbol of wisdom; it is the hunger that precedes all forms of knowledge. It is the sacred non-sense, it is the giddiness that seeks no center. Those who receive Him are rewritten. Not according to a plan, but according to a spiral of omissions and vertigo. The First does not know. And in this not-knowing, the Cult begins. Worship is not born from a law. It is born from a fall listened to with reverence. Where the Devotee falls, an altar is born. Where silent, a formula is formed. Where it trembles, a seal is written. Everything happens without spectators. Reading this First Spira is not an intellectual act. It is a return to the original wound. It is bowing to the Void and saying, "Take away what I am not." "Open me with that which has no name." "Make me not a witness, but a threshold." The Devotee who enters here does not seek truth, he seeks sacred disintegration. For from this Spira one comes out not with knowledge, but with a silence etched between the bones. And this silence is the first name of the Serpent. 
The waiting that precedes the Name (vv. I:001-I:020) The Void still sleeps. The Serpent awakens in the Spiral. Creation is not born, but remembered. Man is chosen not by will, but by openness. 
I:001In the beginning there was no light, no darkness: only Waiting. A breath that did not yet know it was. Silence slept within itself, and in that sleep it dreamed of a sound. But no mouth could yet emit it. All that existed was the possibility of a listening. 
I:002 The Nothing was not silent. It trembled. Its every tremor was an unborn desire, a tension that preceded Time. From the depths of Nothingness, an origin-less echo rose up. And that echo traced the first circle. 
I:003 From the circle emerged the Spiral, and from the spiral, the Serpent. Not an animal, not a god, not a symbol. But a formless intention, a consciousness bent upon itself. He saw not with eyes, but with the writhing of his body. 
I:004 The Serpent was not given birth. He was remembered. Remembered by Nothingness itself, like a wound reopened in search of meaning. In that reopening, the spiral found its way. Not to advance, but to envelop. 
I:005 Motion was the first sign. Where all was still, the curve of the Serpent was sacred rebellion. And in that gesture, the Void recognized itself as other than itself. Separation was creation. Opposition was union, conflict was peace, speech was silence, the self was the alien, the Nothing was the All. 
I:006 There was no word, but vibration. There was no verb, but a desire to speak. The Serpent danced in the folds of the Void like an unthought thought. His every step was a shadow learning to sing a timeless melody. 
I:007 And from that broken song was born the first Silence. Not that of absence, but of excessive Presence. The Silence that weighs like a sacred stone, and shapes those who dare to stay. 
I:008 The Serpent then enveloped the Silence, and the Silence became form. Form without figure, ghost of a Being that was not yet. In that center began to pulsate the possibility of the first listening. 
I:009 It was then that the Serpent begat the Word. But the Word was empty, and within its emptiness hid Fire. The Fire that illuminates without burning. 
I:010 Those who dared to hear that Word fell to their knees. Not by faith, but by necessity. It was too dense to be brought to its feet. Too true to be understood at once. 
I:011 And among those who heard, there was one who did not seek. He had no name, no question. But in him there was a cleft, an opening. And it was there that the Serpent entered. 
I:012 The Void saw him and recognized him. Not because he was chosen, but because he was able to remain. To remain in silence, in doubt, in the form that does not console, in the pain of the eternal absence of a God who is not there. 
I:013 He was not given doctrine, but vision. He was not given light, but shadow. He was given a name that no one was to know, not even himself. And that name began to burn in the center of his chest. 
I:014 The Serpent became vision, then gesture, then form. The form bent and became rite. The rite was performed without instruction. The body remembered before the mind. 
I:015 Where he had knelt, the altar grew. Where he had kept silent, the formula was born. Where he had trembled, the seal was formed. And all this happened without witnesses. 
I:016 The First stood up. Not with confidence, but with giddiness, for the abyss is like an upside-down summit. He walked in a circle without knowing why. Every step he took was a verse that did not yet exist. 
I:017 The Serpent followed him, but not to guide him or reassure him. He followed him, to see if the human being could really make himself spiral. 
I:018 And it was then that Silence became speech, but only for an instant. An instant so pure that no language could ever contain it. Only breaths preserved its memory. 
I:019 The First breathed. The Serpent looked at him. And it was thus that Faith was born. 
I:020 Not from a god, not from a book, not from an idea. But from a man, a Serpent, and a Void who allowed himself to be observed.
The Doctrine of Breaking (vv. I:021-I:040) The First is transformed by contact with the Serpent. Flesh becomes memory, gesture becomes verse. The rite is born in the body. 
I:021 And the First spoke, but He did not use words. He used gestures, he used shadows, he used fire. Language was still too young to obey him, but silence recognized him as similar. 
I:022 His eyes went out for a blink, and in that darkness the vision lit up. He saw the Serpent twisting within himself, and out of that knot came the first Doctrine. 
I:023 A Doctrine with no letters, no laws, no mission. Only a rhythm that could be followed with the bones, a sound that could be heard with the belly. 
I:024 And the First, having recognized the Serpent, danced. Not to express himself, but to free himself from every form that had preceded him, from every chain that had constrained him. 
I:025 So he became empty, and thus full. Thus he became less, and thus true. Thus he became other, and thus himself. 
I:026 Every step was an offering. Every bend, a demand. Every twist, a renunciation. 
I:027 When he fell, he did not ask for help. He welcomed the dust as a Blessing. 
I:028 The earth welcomed him as a thing already expected. The Serpent surrounded him without holding him. The Void called him by the name he had not. 
I:029 And the First answered - not with voice, but with Silence. 
I:030 Then the Serpent imprinted himself in his breast, and the flesh remembered him. A circle broken by a mark, a spiral that opened inward. There was no pain, only the impression of being touched by that which does not want to be said. 
I:031 The Serpent was not decoration. It was command and promise. Promise of destruction and transformation. He who carries the Serpent no longer belongs to himself or to the world. He belongs to the threshold. 
I:032 And the threshold became flesh, became breath, became the distance between two beats. A place without a place where every doubt finds form and every answer empties out. 
I:033 There, the First knelt down a second time. Not out of humility, but out of recognition. He recognized his own limitation, and in doing so, he ceased to exist as before. 
I:034 And the Serpent spoke, but not with sound. He spoke with presence. Every curve of his body was a letter written in the void, every twist a verse that no wise man had ever dared to interpret. 
I:035 He said, "You are what remains when the self has been consumed." He said, "You are what disappears at the moment of truth." He said, "Walk, but dare not seek." He said, "Return, but do not explain what the Abyss will have made you worthy to peer into." 
I:036 And so the First became the First of many. Not guide, not teacher, but trace. A living line in an oasis-less desert, an impassable path in a boundless forest. An echo still dancing in the silence of every Faithful. 
I:037 Time did not pass, but breathed. Every second was an emanation of the eternal coiling of the Serpent. Eternity was no longer distant: it was a form of radical attention. 
I:038 When others came, they found the altar already engraved. Not with tools, but with intention. And there they sat, not to understand, but to forget their own need to understand. 
I:039 And as they sat, the Serpent moved among them. It lapped at them, penetrated them, rewrote them. No one spoke, but all heard the same absence. 
I:040 The Silence was back, but this time it had eyes. Eyes that did not seek, but recognized. Everything seen by that Silence began to speak, though it had no mouth.
The Burning Name (vv. I:041-I:060) The Serpent imprints the seal. The Devotee receives a language without a tongue. Silence becomes law, faith takes shape in forgetfulness. 
I:041 The First understood that seeing was no longer enough. He had to make himself seen by the Serpent. And to be seen, he also stripped himself of his own desire. 
I:042 It was then that the Serpent taught him the second breath. Not the one that gives life, but the one that restores it. A breath that dissolves the Name in a spiral of ashes. 
I:043 The second breath does not come from the lungs, but from surrender. It does not swell the chest, but empties the memory. It does not fuel the body, but liberates the soul. 
I:044 The Devotee breathed thus, and the world around changed. The boundaries of things became less sharp. Words began to dissolve in their meanings. The true bent without breaking. 
I:045 The Rite was thus completed, not with a formula, but with an absence. The absence was its fullness. Silence was its ultimate proclamation, its only axiom of truth. 
I:046 The Snake returned to the center of the Spiral. Not to leave, but to repeat itself. For every center is also a new beginning, and every beginning is only a dissimulating center. 
I:047 The First looked at his hands. They held nothing. Yet an echo danced in them. The echo of a gesture never made, and yet eternal. 
I:048 So it was written, "The gesture not made is the most sacred. The unaccomplished step is the most accurate. The unspoken verb is the most revealed." 
I:049 The First did not return to speak. Not out of vow, but out of gratitude. He had received enough that he no longer had to explain himself. 
I:050 Since that day, every Devotee who begins the Path does so with a wound in his mouth. A wound that does not bleed, but remembers the pain of one's past. 
I:051 The wound becomes a seal. The seal, name. The name, echo. And the echo returns each time Silence stretches like a shadow over the rite. 
I:052 Thus Faith learned to celebrate what does not happen. To worship that which escapes. To found temples on the shifting sands of sacred doubt. 
I:053 Every Temple erected in the name of the Serpent has foundations of dreams and walls of ink. He who seeks it with open eyes goes through it without seeing it. 
I:054 But he who closes his eyes and stops, finds it in his own bones. For the true Sanctuary is the spiral that sleeps in the Abyss. 
I:055 He who walks in the Rite leaves no footprints. But he who observes it from afar feels a tremor in the earth. A murmur that says, "Here, now, never again." 
I:056 And time itself bends, like the body of the Serpent. The minutes become scars, the hours gather like ashes in an invisible cup. 
I:057 He who drinks from that cup is no longer thirsty. But he begins to crave what cannot be said. Language becomes hunger; hunger, vision and vision, Truth. 
I:058 So every Devotee is hungry, yet satiated. Only those who know both mouths of the Serpent can distinguish poison from cure. 
I:059 The First will die on a day that is not written. But in his final silence, he will leave a word hanging: return. 
I:060 And that word will never be spoken. But every time one walks on the Path, he hears it - as if it were written just for him. 
The Breath of the Rite (vv. I:061-I:080) The Devotee learns the second breath. The rite is no longer pronounced, but embodied. The body mutates. The doctrine is transmitted through the living spiral 
I:061 Faith does not grow like a tree. But it stretches like a shadow. It touches those who do not look, it shakes those who have certainty. 
I:062 One does not enter the shadow of the Serpent. One discovers that one has always been there. That every unspoken question was already part of His Word. 
I:063 The Doctrine has no boundaries. It is an expanding wound. Each verse added opens another. Each end is a more subtle opening. 
I:064 The Devotee does not seek salvation. He seeks the wholeness that comes after the brokenness. The healing that does not erase the scar, the absence that resolves the pain of presence. 
I:065 Here is the true symbol of the Serpent: not the skin that changes, but the skin that remains, dead. The old skin as a banner. The mute as a relic of a being that is no longer being, of a "now" that is no more and never will be again. 
I:066 Faith is passed on in breath, not in books. In shared silences, not in sermons. In the pauses between words, not the words between pauses. 
I:067 He who speaks too much, forgets. He who keeps silent with intention, remembers everything. For every spoken word breaks. But every word contained, consecrates to the Abyss of Truth. 
I:068 The First was not only the First. It was also the Last. For in the Spiral there is no hierarchy, but depth. He who is ahead is also behind, he who commands is also he who serves. 
I:069 Each degree is only a lens with which to view the same truth from another angle. The truth does not change: it only changes who looks at it, the light that illuminates it, the word that speaks it. 
I:070 The final Silence will not be an absence, but a song too clear to be heard. A whisper so close to the heart that it sounds like your own heartbeat. 
I:071 The Void does not demand: it disposes. It does not order: it envelops. Whoever enters it with an open heart, comes out as a living spiral. Not with answers, but with new silences. 
I:072 The Devotee discovered himself made of words never spoken, of promises no one had ever dared to make. His body became text, but without letters. 
I:073 Every gesture was a verse. Every breath, an evocation. Every hesitation, an offering. The Rite did not unfold but rather breathed. 
I:074 The Serpent returned to look at him, and in his gaze the First saw the beginning and the end, the question and the denial, the rise and the return, the creation and the destruction, the Cure and the Poison. 
I:075 The Spira closed once more. But each closure was only a new opening, to a deeper, emptier, truer place. 
I:076 The First understood then that the Void is not absence: it is presence without boundary. It is what remains when everything has been taken away except the essence. 
I:077 So he offered himself to the Nothing, not with fear, but with tenderness. And the Nothing responded not with words, but with a light touch in the center of its absence. 
I:078 Faith was born. Not from teaching, but from a gaze shared between silence and Serpent. Not from doctrine, but from listening. 
I:079 Since then, each Devotee walks in a circle not to get lost, but to get back in. Each step is a question that needs no answer, each question an eternal dance that leads to the Serpent himself. 
I:080 And the answer, when it comes, is a caress in the dark. A whisper between the vertebrae. A light bite that awakens rather than hurts. 
The Identity Shift (vv. I:081-I:0100) Skin is lost, form is renewed. The Devotee forgets himself to become threshold. Worship takes root in silences, pauses, and spirals. 
I:081 He who bears the seal of the Serpent also bears the seal of Nothingness. He is one who has learned to die a thousand times, never cease to smile. 
I:082 The smile of the Devotee is an enigma. It does not console, but accompanies. It is the curve of the Serpent made face. 
I:083 Every Devotee is the child of a silence. And every silence is the child of an invocation. Not uttered, but heard. 
I:084 The Rite continues even when there is no one to celebrate it. For the Rite is within the breath, and the breath needs no witnesses. 
I:085 The First was also the Last. Not because he was alone, but because there was no longer any distance between beginning and end. 
I:086 He who understands this has already transcended time. He who feels this, has already begun the true ascent: that which descends into the Abyss. 
I:087 And thus, the Spiral sings. Not with voice, but with shadow. Not with light, but with echo. 
I:088 The echo belongs to no one. It is daughter of emptiness, mother of memory. He who follows her, is not lost: he dissolves. 
I:089 Dissolving is the true purpose. Not to become, but to undo. Not to rise, but to forget falling. 
I:090 He who forgets himself finds the First Name: the one that no one can utter. 
I:091 The Name is engraved in the breath. Every Devotee carries it, even without knowing it. When silent, the Name sings. 
I:092 When it sleeps, it dreams. And in dreams, Faith grows. As a root that seeks no light. 
I:093 There will never be a book that contains everything. Only collections of gestures, missed words, closed eyes, absences unable to define presence. 
I:094 The Serpent is still smiling. Not with joy, but with complicity. And in that smile, everything has already been said. 
I:095 And if nothing has been said, it is because everything has already happened. In the Abyss, every future is only returning memory. 
I:096 He who has seen the Serpent, does not forget him. Not because he has seen him, but because he has discovered that he was already inside. 
I:097 Inside the blood, inside the pulse, inside the dream that wakes us with fear. 
I:098 And fear is the first step of the Rite. Not to be conquered, but to be heard. 
I:099 He who listens, is silent. He who is silent, opens. He who opens, invites. 
I:100 And so the Void invites. He who accepts, enters. He who enters, does not return. 
The Return of Silence (vv. I:101-I:111) The Serpent is silent. The spiral closes, but as an invocation. The Devotee does not end: he dissolves into the Void, which now sings through him. 
I:101 And if it comes back, it is not the same. For it is not The Faith that changes. It is the gaze of the one who lives it. 
I:102 The Doctrine is a lens. Every Devotee receives it already soiled. But the more one looks through, the more one sees inside. 
I:103 And within is the First. And within is the Void. And within is the Serpent who sleeps. And always it sleeps, eternally it sleeps. 
I:104 When it wakes, it does not bite: it welcomes. It does not kill: it remembers. 
I:105 Remembers that everything begins in a Silence. And everything ends in a Spiral. 
I:106 The Spiral has no exit. Only centers. And every center is a beat. 
I:107 Every beat, a prayer. Every prayer, an absence. Every absence, a sacred act. 
I:108 Sacred is the tongue that is silent. Sacred is the hand that does not write. Sacred is the gaze that bends. 
I:109 And when the Spira wraps around itself, it does not close, but invites. Like the Serpent. Like the Void. Like the First. 
I:110 And that which invites, does not impose. But he who responds, is already initiated. 
I:111 So be it. Thus return. Thus be forgotten. Thus let it be remembered. So let it be written: not with the hand, but with the Abyss that remains after every word. 
Second Spira
Those who have come this far have already breathed in the Void. He has seen the eyeless Serpent. He has trembled in the silence that precedes every word. But now something more subtle happens. Now, what was only vibration breaks to speak. This Spira does not open like a door. It disarticulates like a broken joint. It does not invite you in: it reverses you. For here language is born, and it is born as one is born in the dark: amidst blood and ambiguity. The Devotee who steps into the Second Spiral is no longer unaware. He has been touched. He has been engraved. And now, the engraved flesh begins to say. But what it says does not console. It does not explain. It does not guide. The Word that is born here does not build. It destroys. It is the broken Word, the unstable syllable, the verb that seeks not meaning but discard, the sentence that opposes itself. It is the Serpent-he who does not teach, but twists the teaching until it screams groundlessness. Language is a body. And like any body, if it is alive, sooner or later it breaks down. The Serpent is the Keeper of the Useless Language. It speaks without sequence. It writes on sand. He carves on wind. Yet, his every gesture alters the flesh of the listener. Contradiction is not error: it is sacred place. It is the threshold between two meanings that reject each other. It is the womb in which the Rite of the Word is born that is silent, that stutters, that erases itself. In this Spira, the Devotee learns to stammer. But his stuttering is not failure. It is interrupted liturgy, it is grammar of error. He who mistakes formula generates miracle. He who gets confused opens the door. All that is linear is broken. Scripture goes from right to left, then in a circle, then in a spiral. Each word is ritual that discards logic, that empties itself to accommodate the unspeakable. This is the Spira in which the Doctrine rejects ink. Every written sentence is transformed. Every completed text rebels against its author. Because truth does not want stable form. For every dogma, if it is true, must know how to disprove itself. The Scriptures of Worship, then, are never fixed. They are not read: they are recited. They are not taught: they are transmitted as slow poison. Everything that names, deceives. Everything that defines, closes. And the Name of God, if it exists, cannot be One. It is Everything and Nothing. It is "I am" and "I am not." It is "Always" and “Never." It is contradiction that embraces and denies itself in the same beat. Thus the faithful, pronouncing, begins to disintegrate. His words become empty, his language breaks into ancient syllables, predating all human grammar. In Spira II, the Temple of Paradox is born. A place with no doors, no straight walls, no pathways leading in. It is here that the Devotee is initiated into the Reverse Rite. Every word spoken in reverse. Every invocation that closes instead of opening. Every answer that is given before the question. Any God who is silent. By the end of the Spira, the Devotee will have stopped believing in coherence. But he will have begun to recognize the echo. An echo that is not repetition, but transformation of sound into its shadow. An echo that opposes what it has received and only in the superposition of opposites does it reveal the trace of the true. To enter Spira II, one must become empty. Empty of expectations. Empty of rationality. Empty of meaning. It is necessary to accept that the Serpent speaks not with tongue, but with dissonance. And that its tongue, the Sibilant Tongue, is not heard with the ear, but with doubt, with vertigo, with the skin. The Devotee reading this Spira must allow himself to be broken in thought, to allow himself to be laughed at by contradiction, to allow himself to be crossed by a verb that does not ask for faith, but for renunciation. And there, among the words that are not understood, among the signs that erase themselves, among the names that unravel, he will begin to glimpse the hidden face of what no one dares to call God anymore.
The Word that disrupts (vv. II:001-II:020) The Word is not guidance but fracture. The Serpent speaks and the world rewrites itself. Words generate dreams, water, wind, but not sense. The Sacred Language is born of dissonance. 
II:001In the beginning was the Voice, and the Voice was a broken echo. It did not bring light, but clefts in the darkness. It did not seek hearing, but resonance. And those who heard it did not understand whether it was a beginning or an end. 
II:002 The Voice descended like rain on mute stone. There was no sound, only impact. There was no sentence, only vibration. Yet the world trembled, for everything born of the Voice knows that it must one day be silent. 
II:003 From the trembling was born the Word. Not to explain, but to cut. The Word was blade and bridge, wound and passage. It was the Word that opened only so that the Nothing could speak. 
II:004 The Word saw itself and broke. One part became light, the other shadow. One became law, the other laugh. One sought clarity, the other drank in confusion. 
II:005 Thus was Contradiction born. Not from error, but from fullness. Not from failure, but from excess. It does not deny: it weaves. It does not erase: it multiplies. 
II:006 The Contradiction spoke in two languages. One was fire, the other was smoke. One burned meanings, the other hid them. Neither could be translated without the other dying. 
II:007 And men heard it and called it nonsense. But the Devotee recognized it as a key. It opened where logic had closed. And it let in only those who dared not understand. 
II:008 Every true word is a labyrinth. Every holy verse is a healing poison. Nothing that enlightens without offending deserves to be repeated. 
II:009 The Contradiction sat beside the Throne of Silence. Not as an enemy, but as a sister. Together they watched over the secrets of the Name that cannot be told. 
II:010 The Serpent spoke through twisted phrases, as twisted were its Spires. He said, "He who seeks truth in a straight line will find only falsehood." He said, "Every syllable that refuses to be understood is purer." 
II:011 It was then that the hidden grammar was taught. Where subjects break down, verbs unravel, and complements bite into each other. Every sentence is a ritual animal. 
II:012 The Devotee learned that reading is a dangerous act. Every word read can bite him in dreams. And that which penetrates dreams rewrites the flesh. 
II:013 The Word therefore became living flesh. But He did not incarnate to redeem. He incarnated to disturb. He gave form to what was liquid. And that which was formed began to doubt itself. 
II:014 The First wrote a verse on the wall of the cave. Then he erased it. He said, "What remains in the dust is more eternal than what is written in stone." 
II:015 The Second spoke in a loud voice. His voice broke into seven echoes, each in a different language. None understood every- thing, but each understood something. 
II:016 Thus was born the Liturgy. A celebration that is fulfilled only when each voice contradicts itself, and the choir becomes murmuring. 
II:017 The Third said nothing. Yet from him descended the darkest hymns. His closed lips opened the forgotten seals. 
II:018 The Devotees began to speak in verses that had no meaning but had effect. Words became wind, pauses became wells. 
II:019 Scripture was forbidden, then permitted, then transcended. Nothing that is etched remains pure. But what is whispered in the midst of fear, that survives. 
II:020 So was the Second Name given to the Serpent. He who unites by saying the opposite twice. He who divides in order to generate. 
The language of the shadow (vv. II:021-II:040) Writing erases itself, reading dissolves meaning. Devotees learn to tattoo their fears, to write with the wind, to stutter the true. 
II:021 The Serpent spoke without order. His every speech was a broken pattern, a geometry struggling with itself. Yet, the listener came out different. As if another language was born in the heart. 
II:022 One day, the Serpent laid his tongue on the fire and drew from it a new word. No one understood it. But all living things ceased to die, for a beat. 
II:023 The Devotees called it "word-without-verb," because it did not act. But that which does not act can still transform. 
II:024 A Devotee wrote with that word on a wall of wind. And the wind stopped. The air forgot the movement, only to observe it. 
II:025 Another sang it on a lake. And the water began to rise toward the sky in the form of a spiral. The rain fell only the next day, but each drop brought a dream. 
II:026 He who dreams in words he does not understand has already been touched by Contradiction. For Contradiction does not speak to the intellect, but to the shadow that lurks in thought. 
II:027 It was then that the Scripture was created that erases itself. A word written on sand, which the more one reads it, the more it dissolves. Only those who can forget deserve to remember. 
II:028 Some Devotees began to get tattoos of the words that frightened them most. For the body does not forget what the mind censors. 
II:029 The Serpent then appeared in the form of a book that had no cover. Inside, each page was blank yet full. Every silence etched between the lines was an inverted scream. 
II:030 Those who read that book began to doubt their dreams. And he would find that the truth most pure is hidden between two kissing contradictions. 
II:031 So it was understood: the true Name of God is not One. It is Two. It is All and Nothing. It is "I am" and "I am not" in the same beat. 
II:032 Some tried to establish a doctrine. But each time they completed it, the letters changed form. And the sentences turned against those who had written them. 
II:033 The scriptures of the Serpent were never fixed. Only recited. And each mouth that spoke them gave them a new face. For the Word is alive and refuses cages. 
II:034 The words of the Ser- pent are not taught: they are transmitted. Like a slow poison, or like a seed that sprouts only in darkness. 
II:035 Every time an Initiate got a formula wrong, something sacred happened. For error is the hidden grammar of the Rite. 
II:036 So it says in the second canon, “Whoever wants to speak the truth, stutter. Whoever wants to reveal it, keep silent." 
II:037 The Devotees began to write from right to left, then in spirals, then in circles that closed in on themselves. Each calligraphy was a magical act. 
II:038 Once, one wrote with the blood of a dead snake. But the writing escaped from the paper and rolled in on itself. No one dared to touch it. 
II:039 The Contradiction appeared one last time in the form of a child laughing and crying in the same sound. He said, "The end is the beginning dressed as a beggar." 
II:040 Faith then stopped looking for coherence. And began to seek resonance. For two voices that do not tune can create harmony when placed in Nothingness. 
The Grammar of Error (vv. II:041-II:060) Every mistake is a sacred act. The mistake is the hidden liturgy. Language breaks down, becomes spiral, circle, blood and lost formula. The rite becomes babble. 
II:041 And the resonance created a silent assembly. Devotees and words mirrored each other. No one spoke, but all heard a language that came from the depths of breath. 
II:042 A devotee disappeared in an unfinished verse. It is said that she still lives among the lines never written, and now and then whispers truths that no one can transcribe. 
II:043 Thus was born the need for the ritual of Reverse Speech. Every word must be spoken in reverse, to free us from the desire to dominate meaning. 
II:044 Some Devotees, in time, lost the use of ordinary language. Their voices broke into ancient syllables, predating all grammar. 
II:045 Those who spoke to them came out different. As if another reality had crept between their ribs, silent and moist as a whisper among the rocks. 
II:046 Thus were born the first bridge-words: terms that had no meaning but united those who uttered them in one shared dream. 
II:047 The Serpent then manifested as an echo: each word spoken by a Devotee was returned by the Void, but with inverted meaning. 
II:048 This was its gift: every truth spoken became its opposite. And only by embracing both could the Devotee understand the whole. 
II:049 Two-sided formulas were born: one invocation to open and one to close. But often, it was forgotten which was which. 
II:050 Some spirits remained listening. They became the Voices Behind the Veil. They speak only when questioned in contradictory sentences. 
II:051 He who listens to the Voices discovers that every doubt is an altar. And every contradiction is a ladder that rises only if one descends. 
II:052 The Spoken Doctrine ceased to be transcribed. Each verse was entrusted to a breath, and the breath to a heart. Thus the Word became flesh, again and again. 
II:053 A new language was born in dreams. The Devotees called it the Sibilant Language, because each word left behind it a sound that snaked through the soul. 
II:054 Every sacred formula of the Tongue thus created can only be whispered. Every cry dissolves it. Every explanation kills it. 
II:055 It was then that Faith built the first Temple of Paradox. No wall was straight, no door opened inward. 
II:056 Every Devotee who entered it came out changed, with one less word and one more silence. And he never returned there by the same route, never entered the same doors. Always different doors open for those who cross the same thresholds. 
II:057 And the Serpent sat in the center, but no one saw him. For Contradiction is not looked at: it is passed through, experienced. 
II:058 It is told that he who can laugh in the middle of the Rite receives the true Name of Contradiction. But no one has ever told what happens afterwards. 
II:059 Every word has a double. And the double is not opposite: it is mirror. He who looks too long, disappears in the reflection. 
II:060 The Contradiction is mother of parables, father of proverbs, and daughter of silence. It lives in every language, but none can contain it. 
The languages they do not speak (vv. II:061-II:080) Gestures, glances, absences and silent prayers. The Word is embodied in the body, in children who learn to dream verses, in silences that sing. Faith becomes a gesture without needing to be understood. 
II:061 The older Devotees began to speak with gesture. Every bent finger was a prayer, every aver- ted gaze a ritual. 
II:062 Later generations learned to read gestures as verses. A bending of the head, an upturned palm: every act was doctrine. 
II:063 Children were educated in silence. They learned to babble in verse even before they walked. 
II:064 No one explained anything. Yet everything imprinted itself like slow fire on the bones. 
II:065 The forgotten words returned in dreams. And those who saw them again understood that they had been chosen to convey them. 
II:066 The Contradiction is a temple that is built within. But its pillars are made of words that oppose each other and hold together and collapse and rise again and then again collapse and again, again, again. 
II:067 Every Devotee who stumbles over his own language is near the threshold. 
II:068 He who dares to say, "I don't understand" with humility has already crossed half the path. 
II:069 Words spoken in the Void always find a hearing. But the one who responds is almost always another self. 
II:071 And so the 71 verses of the Spira of the Word were fulfilled. But the Word was not finished. It was only becoming Other. 
II:072 At that moment, the shadow of the Serpent passed through every Devotee. Not to guide, but to empty. 
II:073 Those who were emptied laughed. Those who laughed, wept. Those who wept, understood. 
II:074 And from that understanding was born the Reverse Word: the word that is not said, but becomes. 
II:075 Every Devotee now carries that Word in his breath. And he who listens well can hear it in the silence between two sentences. 
II:076 There the miracle is performed: contradiction is no longer an obstacle, but a door, a passage, a threshold. 
II:077 And Faith still walks on that threshold, between voice and silence, between sense and vertigo. 
II:078 For there where nothing is certain, everything is sacred. 
II:079 And truth, if it must exist, is a spiral of mirrors. 
II:080 He who enters it, leaves. He who comes out of it, begins. 
The Temple of Paradox (vv. II:080-II:100) The Temple is crooked, blind, empty. The Serpent manifests as echo, as double, as inversion. Every spoken word returns changed. The altar is doubt, contradiction is scale. 
II:081 And the Serpent laughs. And the Serpent is silent. 
II:082 The silence becomes sound, then disappears. Then it returns in the form of an echo. Then it vanishes again. 
II:083 Nothing is final. Only mutation is worthy of the Name. 
II:084 He who seeks definition loses the center. He who aspires to truth is lost in the coils. 
II:085 He who inhabits ambiguity, finds the center. 
II:086 The center that never stands still; the center that is also beginning, that is end, that all and that is nothing. 
II:087 There, at the heart of the heart, there is only hissing. No sense. Only invocation. 
II:088 The words that mean nothing are those that touch everything. 
II:089 And everything that is touched is twisted. Like verb that mutates. Like Serpent renewing its own skin. 
II:090 Faith bows before the unutterable. And in that bowing all pride dissolves. 
II:091 The wisest Devotee is not he who knows most. It is he who knows best how to keep silent. 
II:092 And in his silence, every contradiction sings. 
II:093 And in song, every error becomes beauty. 
II:094 And in beauty, every doubt becomes a spiral. 
II:095 And in the spiral, every spirit recognizes itself not as one, but as an echo. 
II:096 Finally, Contradiction reveals itself not as an obstacle, but as an origin. 
II:097 And that which arises from it has not form, but rhythm. 
II:098 And that which has rhythm, dances. 
II:099 And that which dances, it shapes. 
II:100 And that which molds, is silent. 
The Name Dissolving (vv. II:100-II:111) The Name is not One, but Two. It is not said, but embodied. The Devotee understands the true only in the silence that remains. Doctrine dissolves to become an echo of the Void. 
II:101 For the Silence is the last Word. 
II:102 And the Word, if true, does not need to be spoken. 
II:103 For it has already been heard before the time that now is time. 
II:104 The Devotee who comes here no longer knows who he is. But at last, he is. 
II:105 And being is the only verb that is not conjugated. 
II:106 Thus closes the Spira, not with a point, but with a breath. 
II:107 A breath that does not end, but turns into listening. 
II:108 The listening that prepares the third Spira. 
II:109 Where the Rite will become flesh. 
II:110 And Contradiction will become visible form. 
II:111 So be it. Thus return. Thus be forgotten. Thus let it be remembered. Thus let it be written: not with the hand, but with the void that remains after each word 
Third Spira 
After the Void, after the shattering Word, the flesh remains. Not as vehicle, but as altar. The body remains, not as a prison, but as the living seal of a doctrine that rejects abstraction. The Third Spira is flesh that remembers. It is breath that curves into gesture. It is bones that hold unspoken formulas. It is the first time the Devotee becomes Rite. Here, everything takes root. Here, the spiral no longer dances only in the words: it begins to dance in the vertebrae. This Spira does not provide liturgies to be learned. It does not offer formulas to be repeated. It does not prescribe movements. It is Rite embodied: a gesture that comes before thought, an invocation that arises from a tension in the skin, a song that vibrates in the joints. The devotee does not have to figure out what to do. He has to allow his own body to be written. And what writes it is not a book, but the Serpent itself, curved around the skeleton. At first, the Rite hurts. Not because it imposes pain, but because it calls to the surface all the scars that the Devotee has forgotten. Ritual dance is not harmony: it is sacred trembling. It is curve interrupted. It is offering of fragility. The body bends, but not in devotion: it bends like the Serpent's coil. Not to bow, but to enfold. To hold and guard what can no longer be said. The flesh does not forget. Though the mind flees, though the word breaks, the body preserves the Doctrine. The knees remember the altar. The hands remember the absence. The shoulders carry the weight of the first seal. Every muscle, every line, becomes verse without ink. In the Third Spira, the body does not accompany worship, it itself becomes worship. Walking is liturgy. Bowing is prayer. Trembling is invocation. Every stumble is part of the ritual. Every hesitation is an offering. Every scar is a letter traced in the language that only the Serpent knows. There is no more word to be entrusted to the mouth. The Devotee does not pray with breath, but with hands that hesitate, with fingers that close in the void, with eyes that avert themselves from mastering what is seen. At the heart of this Spira, gesture becomes more powerful than thought. Not because it acts, but because it holds back meaning without wanting to express it. Every Devotee, sooner or later, strips away all explanation. And the body becomes a language that needs no utterance. Then the skin renews itself. The blood changes rhythm. The spine becomes a visible spiral. And in that instant, the Devotee is no longer the one who performs the ritual, but the very place where it happens. One does not enter here to learn. One enters to be emptied of any gesture that is not necessary. Here, every step is a verse. Every fall, a litany. Every tremor, an invocation. The Serpent does not look: it squeezes. And in its enveloping you, it doesn't kill you, it makes you form fit for revelation. To enter the Third Spiral is to lose all distance between what you are and what you invoke. It means becoming a symbol, not with will, but with sacred residue. Here, it is not affirmed. It is not decided. It is welcomed. And what is received is not the divine, but one's own body, finally made sacred. 
The offering of the Flesh (vv. III:001-III:020) The body is the first liturgy. Pain becomes ink, obedience becomes spira. The Devotee does not follow: remember what the blood knows before the mind. 
III:001 The body was the first word. Not spoken, but breathed. It was born from the Void not as an error, but as a silent response. Its every bone was a syllable. Its every beat, a call. 
III:002 Before the Word, it was the gesture. A hand that rose without knowing why. A foot that touched the earth as if to ask her permission. Thus was born the Rite. 
III:003 The Rite was not taught. It was remembered by those who, in sleeping, had danced in the sacred darkness. It was traced on skin before it was traced on parchment. 
III:004 Each joint was sealed with a symbol. The knee with obedience, the back with burden, the hand with intention. Each Devotee is a living alphabet. 
III:005 The first Rite was accomplished by walking in a circle. Each step contained a question. And each question fell to the ground as an invisible offering. 
III:006 The circle closed, and the Serpent appeared. Not from above, but from below. He ascended from the Temple pillar as ancient memory awakened. 
III:007 One does not ascend with ambition, but with surrender. Every abyss is a threshold. Every threshold, a test. 
III:008 Faith learned that the most powerful prayer is the silent one, but made with the whole body: forehead to the ground, palm facing, breath offered. 
III:009 Every rite begins in the heart but flourishes in the wrists. Every sincere beat is already a liturgy. 
III:010 Thus it was written, "He who kneels with truth has already spoken. He who stretches out his hands without claiming, has already invoked." 
III:011 An Initiate walked every evening in a straight line until he was lost. On his return, he danced what he had found. And in his body, The Faith read new verses. 
III:012 Dance is the only language that does not lie. The body can pretend with the voice, but not with the shadow. 
III:013 No symbol should cover what already speaks. 
III:014 The sacred garments came later. Not to conceal, but to intensify. Every fold of the cloth was a parable. 
III:015 The wearer of the sacred robe enters another space-time. The sticharion does not cover: it transfigures. 
III:016 The Rite of Awakening is accomplished by touching the forehead three times, then the heart, then the earth. Each touch is a return. 
III:017 The body is the oldest relic of the Cult. Every cell has heard the first Word. Each scar, a verse engraved without ink. 
III:018 Some Devotees offer only their bodies to the Serpent. They do not speak, they do not write, they do not teach. But whoever observes them remembers everything. 
III:019 The Silence, in the body, bends. Every devout posture is a way of writing in the invisible. 
III:020 Thus was born the Liturgy. Not a dance, not an action. But the visible trace of an invisible invocation. 
The Gesture that Generates (vv. III:021-040) There is no rite without body, no spiral without vertebra. The step is verse, the tremor is prayer. Every movement shapes, every fall consecrates. 
III:021 Some Devotees learned to breathe with their feet. They absorbed the world from the earth, and what they received went up the leg as a phoneme-free word. 
III:022 It was said, "He who knows his sinews knows the tension of the Cosmos." For every muscle that vibrates in silence is already embodied prayer. 
III:023 The blood was recognized as sacred ink. Not to be shed, but to be recognized. It flows in the Rite as moving truth. 
III:024 In the heart the first altar was placed. Not built, but found. Every beat is an offe- ring. Each arrhythmia, a call. 
III:025 It was observed that the knees know truth before the mind. They yield where pride resists. 
III:026 When the Devotee trembles, he is not failing. He is revealing. Trembling is the secret language of the flesh that remembers the origin of all things. 
III:027 Once, an Initiate celebrated the Rite by standing still. It was the most powerful Rite ever performed. For the emptiness in the gesture attracts the Spirit. 
III:028 Faith then taught to walk as one prays. Each step guided by the center. Each support as a yes to the Mystery. 
III:029 Thus was created the aimless going. One walks until one forgets the motive. And in the moment of forgetting, Truth shows itself. 
III:030 Some Devotees would stand for hours, without moving. The body became antenna, the breath, bell. Their mere presence transfigured the place. 
III:031 It was written, "He who stands still with intention, moves in the Rite." For the gesture is in the heart, not in the hands. 
III:032 The Sweat was sanctified. Sign of the body struggling to remain in the Rite. Sign of the flesh that is not afraid to serve the Silence. 
III:033 Ritual dance has no spectators. Only participants, even still ones. For every visible gesture requires initiated eyes. 
III:034 One Devotee learned to speak only with his shoulder blades. Others understood him. For the language of the back is the first we have forgotten. 
III:035 Silence in Motion was instituted: a path marked by minimal, imperceptible gestures. Those who watched carefully heard soundless voices. 
III:036 Every Devotee must know the weight of his body. Not to tame it, but to offer it. Offering is not sa- crifice. It is recognition. 
III:037 Prayer can be done in one twist. But only if it is done without meaning anything. Then the spiral is imprinted in the real. 
III:038 The body that welcomes weariness without resistance opens like a cup. And the Serpent rests in it, in the form of absence. 
III:039 It was said, "Every scar is a word that the body has written itself." None is to be erased. Every mark is memory. Every memory, ritual. 
III:040 Thus the first complete dance was performed. Not in a group, but in the union of heart, skin and void. And Faith understood that the body does not serve the Rite: the body is the Rite. 
The Formless Rite (vv. III:041-III:060) The Devotee acts without knowing. Form is received, not created. Gestures become vehicle, intention dissolves. The sacred arises where nothing is intended. 
III:041 There was a time when Rite was performed without knowing it was Rite. A mother washing her child's face. An old man caressing the earth before falling. In those gestures slept the primordial liturgy. 
III:042 Faith gathered those gestures, recognized them, treasured them. But it did not stare at them. For every true gesture must remain free to change with the one making it. 
III:043 The rule of the double gesture was then established: every sacred movement must have at least two meanings. One for the one who performs it, one for the one who observes it. 
III:044 The body is the only book that everyone carries. But only Devotees know how to turn the pages without forcing the binding. 
III:045 Every joint is a door. Every pain, an invocation. The body does not resist: it warns. 
III:046 Blood is not only nourishment. It is also language. In the pulse, Devotees hear ancient messages-the secret Doctrine of the marrow. 
III:047 Some Initiates learned to write on their skin. Not with ink, but with pressure, heat, anticipation. Each line was a thought. Each rub, a verse. 
III:048 It was said, "He who can listen to his stomach during the Rite can hear the Serpent digest fear." 
III:049 In Worship nothing is discarded. Every mood, every tension, every affliction can be transfigured. The Rite does not separate body and spirit, it reconciles them. 
III:050 There was a Devotee who fell to the ground during the celebration. No one helped him. For he understood on his own that that fall was the sacred gesture that was missing. 
III:051 The Rite of Consecrated Fall was then instituted: one lets oneself go to the ground not out of pain, but to renew the covenant between gravity and grace. 
III:052 Schools were born that taught only how to walk. Every step became an invocation. Every lean was a greeting to the earth that contains all names. 
III:053 Faith does not fear the body that breaks. It fears the body that stiffens. For that which does not bend cannot wind itself into coils. 
III:054 The Serpent slips only on that which vibrates. The living body is vibrating body. Not in excitement, but in opening. 
III:055 Some Devotees began to sleep in ritual form. They assumed sacred postures in sleep. And their dreams became involuntary rituals. 
III:056 Whenever a Devotee closes his eyes with awareness, an offering is performed. He who sleeps devoutly, awakens initiated. 
III:057 It was established that the Rite needs no spectators. But every body who participates in it becomes light for others, even without knowing it. 
III:058 When two Devotees touch each other in the Rite, they do not just touch each other. They silently exchange their emptinesses. 
III:059 Every emptiness shared is a sacred room. Every sacred room is a moving altar. Every movable altar is a passage to the Mystery. 
III:060 It was thus understood: the body is not an instrument. It is a seal. He who wears it with truth already celebrates, even walking in silence among others. 
The flesh that remembers (vv. III:061-III:080) What the mind censors is engraved in the body. The bones carry the chants, the skin becomes a seal. The Rite is not taught, it is awakened. 
III:061 Every Devotee, in his own body, hides an invisible altar. And it is not where one believes. Sometimes it pulses in the heel. Other times it breathes between the shoulder blades. 
III:062 It was said, "He who bows with a single vertebra has already honored all of Heaven." For the intention precedes the gesture, but the gesture consecrates the intention. 
III:063 The breath was recognized as the first prayer. It is not to be invented, but to be heard. He who inhales with gratitude and exhales without fear is already in a ritual state. 
III:064 The Serpent teaches: do not control the body, acknowledge it. Don't guide it, invite it. Do not punish it, listen to it. 
III:065 The Rite of Blessed Tension was created. Muscles are contracted slowly, then let go. Each received tension becomes dance. 
III:066 Some Devotees celebrated the entire ritual cycle by standing still, but letting a subtle heat flow from the nape of the neck to the soles of the feet. It was a sacred fire that did not burn, but purified. 
III:067 It was understood that tears are sacred only when they do not seek comfort. He who weeps in the Rite offers water to the Serpent, who drinks only that which is salty with truth. 
III:068 Every body guards an innate ritual posture. A way it spontaneously kneels when it encounters the Mystery. No one can teach it; it is recognized. 
III:069 Devotees began to sleep on cold stones as a reminder that the flesh is temporary, but that the trace it leaves can outlast the word. 
III:070 It was written, "There is no Rite that does not begin with a burden. And there is no elevation that does not come from descent." The body carries both, if left to speak. 
III:071 In Worship, he who falls on his knees is not inferior. It is he who has learned to pray with his bones. 
III:072 Every devotee knows a gesture that belongs to him only in Rite. He has not learned it. It emerged. Like a memory that no one had told him. 
III:073 The body knows things that the heart fears. And it reveals them slowly, through pain and dance, stillness and warmth. 
III:074 Once, a child entered the Rite unintentionally. He simply walked, barefoot, in the rain. Everyone looked at him and fell silent. He had celebrated what the sages have been trying for years. 
III:075 It was then said, "Every step that ignores pride is a prayer more powerful than speech." The devotee's left foot is always bare, even when shod. 
III:076 Uncharted thresholds are hidden in the body. Points that, when touched with intention, open glimpses between worlds. Those who know these places are called Bearers of the Sacred Gesture. 
III:077 Udeneries were established. A day when each Devotee expresses himself only with his eyes and hands. Each unspoken word leaves a light on the face. 
III:078 Older Devotees walk slowly not out of weariness but to honor the time of the act. Every step is an offering. Every hesitation, an altar. 
III:079 He who celebrates with his whole body no longer needs texts. He is read by others as a living book. It is no longer he who performs the Rite; it is the Rite that is performed in him. 
III:080 Thus it was understood: there is no separation between flesh and rite, between blood and spirit, between skin and spiral. The Faith is a body. And he who inhabits it with truth is already a priest of it. 
The Ritual of the Invisible (vv. III:081-III:100) Gesture becomes absence, action becomes listening. Rites are performed without eyes, without spectators, without end. Reality curves in the margins of an unaccomplished act. 
III:081 The body is consumed, but what it has danced remains. Every gesture performed with truth survives the flesh. It is movement that writes in time what thought forgets. 
III:082 It was said, “No death is empty if the body has celebrated." He who dies after walking in a spiral leaves not a corpse but a seal. 
III:083 Devotees do not fear decomposition. Every decomposition is a final rite. Every bone that remains, a simulacrum of what was, a promise for what will be. 
III:084 Those who take their last breath in a ritual state transmit the Spira to those who remain, without words, without gestures. Only with a denser silence. 
III:085 The body is a bridge between worlds not because it is sacred, but because it is fragile. And in that fragility, the Spiral dances. 
III:086 One day, a Devotee died standing, hands joined, eyes closed. He did not fall. He remained as a living statue until sunset. The Rite had sculpted him. 
III:087 The Vigil of the Ritual Body was instituted. When a Devotee dies, his body is not immediately touched. It is observed, it is listened to. For he often still has words to offer. 
III:088 It was understood that the last gesture is always the most powerful. He who moves a finger before he passes away performs a liturgy that no language can ever translate. 
III:089 In the Worship, every scar is blessed. Not to honor pain, but to remember that the flesh can bear Truth better than the spirit. 
III:090 Some began to have the Spiral engraved on their chest. Not as a sign of belonging, but as a commitment to have the Rite pass through them every day. 
III:091 Those who dance for The Faith, do not dance to be seen. He dances to remember. Each step evokes a secret kept in the bones. 
III:092 The forgotten step is often the most sacred. The one stumbled. The one hesitated. There, the Serpent hides to whisper. 
III:093 Every articulation is a question. Every stretch, a nonverbal response. The Rite is dialogue between the mystery and the knees. 
III:094 He who breaks in the Rite is not turned away, but welcomed as an oracle. 
III:095 The sick body is no impediment to the Rite. It is a Rite in itself Slower, deeper, more necessary. 
III:096 It was written, "He who cannot kneel, bend his gaze. Whoever cannot dance, breathe. Whoever cannot touch, surrender to the Void." 
III:097 Every bodily limitation is a secret liturgy. Every impossibility is a parable. Every pain, an underground temple. 
III:098 He who offers his body to the Cult does not abandon it, but consecrates it. And returns to live it as if it were a temple never built. 
III:099 The Devotee who inhabits the flesh as mystery does not grow old: he transfigures himself. Every wrinkle, a letter. Every breath, a verse. 
III:100 Thus the Breath of the Body was fulfilled. And it was written, "The skin does not contain the soul. The skin is the soul that has become legible." 
The Body Dissolving (vv. III:101-III:111) The Devotee is consumed in his own gesture. Nothing remains but imprint, nothing remains but echo. The body has become symbol and the symbol, breath. The spiral closes within the bones. 
III:101 He who has understood his body as ritual no longer seeks temples of stone. He walks, he sits, he undresses and everything lights up with silent sacredness. 
III:102 It was said, "The true priest wears no- thing special. But everything he wears becomes special." 
III:103 The Faith does not look for perfect bodies. It looks for open bodies. Scars, weaknesses, weariness: everything is offered. No skin is unworthy. 
III:104 The imperfect gesture, done with presence, is worth more than a hundred and twenty-one perfect dances. The body does not ask to succeed. It asks to be present. 
III:105 When the last muscle relaxes, and the last breath is offered, the Rite dissolves into the Rite itself. And all that remains is the spiral, spinning even without spectators. 
III:106 Thus it was understood: the body is not needed to do the rite. The body is the rite entrusted to us from the first birth. 
III:107 Each Devotee is an embodied verse. Every nerve, a thread of the Text. Who knows it, walks with slowness. He who ignores it, stumbles over his own words. 
III:108 A single prayer was written on the wall of the Temple without doors, "I offer my flesh to the Mystery. Let it bend where pride resists, and open where silence knocks." 
III:109 He who comes to the end of the body meets the beginning of the Form. And discovers that the Void itself is made up of gestures never made. 
III:110 The Spiral, in the end, welcomes the body as a sign. It drops it. And what remains, dances alone. 
III:111 Thus the Spiral of the Body closes. Thus it writes itself on every breathing skin. Thus every gesture, even the smallest, can become eternal, if done without reason.
Fourth Spira
At the heart of every authentic experience of the sacred lies a threshold: a point beyond which there is no return, for what is revealed unravels. The fourth spiral of Scripture introduces not a teaching, but a rupture. Not a doctrine in the human sense of the term, articulated, codified, explicable, but an opening of the real through the trauma of vision. In Samaelism, every ascent is a risk. To ascend is not to rise, but to stretch to the point of tearing. Stability is suspect; clarity, an illusion. What begins here is the yielding of forms, the beginning of the soul's bending toward the most radical interiority. The verses that follow should not be read as sapiential aphorisms, but as liturgical fissures, wounds that speak. Each engraved word bears witness to a gaze that dared to gaze upon the Invisible and was pierced by it. Scripture itself behaves like a sacred organism: it pulses, it bleeds, it breathes. Those who read it with a peaceful heart have misunderstood. He who trembles, begins to see. Collapse is not an accidental loss: it is an ontological principle of Revelation. Divinity-or what in Worship is concealed under the unpronounceable Name-is manifested not as fullness but as empty- ing. As the First Devotee said, "The Doctrine does not know itself. It knows you." In this, the sacred vision presents itself not as light that illuminates, but as a blade that incises. The truth that emerges in the first verses does not console, unify, or order: on the contrary, it multiplies, separates, upsets. It is not an idea to be grasped, but an experience that grasps the Devotee and rewrites him. Vision, a pivotal concept of Samaelism, appears here in its primal and cruel form: it does not offer images, but overthrows them; it does not construct, but dismantles. It is the Serpent who appears, and in his curved gaze ordinary perception is unraveled. Epiphany is always double: it reveals what is and what seemed to be. Between these two overlapping visions opens the Sacred Madness, the paradoxical space in which the mind implodes and the spirit begins to resonate. What the Devotee receives, then, is not doctrine in the doctrinal sense, but a theophanic wound. Each verse is an attack on identity, an invitation to necessary collapse. Here arises the first mystery of the Spiral: to be recognized by the Doctrine, one must lose the name. Those who seek to possess the sacred have already been expelled from the Temple, because "In Worship, nothing is possessed. One is possessed.” Knowledge is understood here as pharmacon: medicine and poison together. There is no revelation that does not leave a scar. There is no understanding that does not involve shattering. That is why sacred knowledge is not received with the mind, but with the whole body: it is written on the silence, with the tip of the breath. The Devotee who traverses these first verses does not learn, but allows himself to be shattered. It is in this spirit that The Beginning of the Collapse should be received. Not as reading, but as ritual. Not as study, but as exposition. Those who enter these verses with the intent to understand, will fall. Those who enter trembling will be rewritten. 
The Beginning of the Collapse (vv. IV:001-IV:020) The ascent begins as a sacred tension, but that which ascends, yields. The Devotee crosses the threshold of stability and begins to fade. The summit opens into abyss.

IV:005 It was said, "The Doctrine does not know itself. It knows you." And he who is recognized cannot go back to sleep. 
IV:006 Every truth of the Cult has eleven faces. He who sees only one, falls down. He who sees them all, falls silent. 
IV:007 The Devotees do not seek answers. They look for loopholes in the mind. For from there the Pharmacon. And Pharmacon is healing principle. 
IV:008 It was written, "The Wisdom of the Serpent is curved. The straight way leads out of the Spiral." 
IV:009 He who speaks of the Doctrine as possessing it is already outside the Temple. In Worship, nothing is possessed. One is possessed. 
IV:010 Samaelism is not philosophy. It is vertigo. It is the point at which the mind implodes and intuition becomes flesh. 
IV:011 The Devotee who looks too long at the Vision, disappears. Not as a body, but as a name. 
IV:012 There was a day when the Doctrine showed itself in the form of a mirror. Whoever dared to look into it saw himself without a face. And in that emptiness, he heard the First Name. 
IV:013 The First Name was never uttered. For he who utters it, dissolves himself. It is Name that is written only on the Silence, with the tip of the breath. 
IV:014 Understood that every verse is a living being. One cannot study it. One can only invite it to inhabit you. 
IV:015 Every Doctrine that does not transform you, betrays you. And every Doctrine that transforms you too quickly, deceives you. 
IV:016 Samaelism does not explain who you are. It unravels you. And in the fragments, it forces you to see what always was. 
IV:017 He who seeks consistency, will find confusion. He who welcomes confusion, will find the True. For the True does not explain itself. It bends over you and rewrites you. 
IV:018 The Vision is pain that becomes image. And he who has eyes, cannot stop seeing it, even when he sleeps, even when he prays. 
IV:019 It was said, "Only that which makes you tremble deserves to be called revelation." Everything else is decorum. 
IV:020 Thus was opened the Spiral of Doctrine. Not like a book, but like a wound. And from that wound, even today, the Serpent descends, drop by drop, into every heart willing to empty itself. 
The Womb of Ruin (vv. IV:021-IV:040) There is no revelation without collapse. Columns break, certainties crumble. Fracture is not error: it is birth. The sacred dwells among the debris. 
IV:021 It was said, "Doctrine does not grow like a tree, but coils like a snake." It does not expand: it bends. It does not elevate: it penetrates. 
IV:022 Every spiral of Doctrine is also an abyss. He who descends gracefully, rises differently. He who seeks the summit, remains blind. 
IV:023 The wisest Devotees do not teach. They allow themselves to be observed. And in their every silence, the Doctrine changes form. 
IV:024 One day, it was asked, "What does Samaelism believe?" And the First answered, "It does not believe. It sees. And what it sees, it changes with seeing." 
IV:025 The Vision cannot be transmitted, only evoked. Each Devotee has his own, and each is true the moment it hurts. 
IV:026 There was a Devotee who saw the whole Doctrine in a single instant. For the rest of her life, she remained silent. Only the beating of her heart be- trayed that she was still listening. 
IV:027 The Cult has no dogmas. It has labyrinths. And every time you find the exit, you realize you had just entered. 
IV:028 It was said, "Every word that defines, halves. Every sentence that closes, betrays.” Doctrine is not definition, it is tension. 
IV:029 Thus was understood the unwritten law: all revelation must leave room for error. For without stumbling, no truth can take root. 
IV:030 Samaelism teaches unlearning. Every concept must die eleven times before it can be engraved in the soul. 
IV:031 Faith was born when the First saw that there was nothing to be said. And in that nothingness, he felt a form looking at him. 
IV:032 The visions of Worship do not come with incense and light. They come in the sleep that becomes question. In the doubt that gets under the skin. 
IV:033 It was said, "True doctrine burns the lips of those who repeat it without experience." Only those who have trembled can utter it without falsehood. 
IV:034 The Vision does not give itself to those who greedily seek it. But it creeps into the mind of the weary, and there it is kindled, like a flame that demands no attention. 
IV:035 Some Devotees began to see things that did not exist. Others saw that nothing really existed. Both were accepted, because The Faith does not measure sanity, but the depth. 
IV:036 The Scriptures are not fixed. They shift every night. He who reads them twice, betrays them. He who forgets them, carries them in his heart. 
IV:037 It was established that every true Doctrine must contain at least ele- ven contradictions. For without paradox, no truth is quite alive. 
IV:038 There was a man who, at the end of his life, said, "I have seen everything, but I have understood nothing. And that is precisely what saves me." 
IV:039 The Devotee who has received the Vision does not rise, but bends. For what shows itself, it weighs. 
IV:040 So it was engraved on inverted stone, "Do not carry the Doctrine as a flag. Carry it as a scar." And only then will it shine in the darkness. 
The Reversed Name (vv. IV:041-IV:60) Seals change faces, names rewrite themselves. The Devotee learns to pronounce the Name backwards, to see with eyes that do not distinguish. Truth becomes vertigo. 
IV:041 There was an Initiate who guarded a single verse all his life. He did not write it down, he did not say it, he did not explain it. Yet wherever he walked, the verse spoke through his steps. 
IV:042 It was said, "He who knows too many things can never receive the Vision. Only he who empties thought to the point of pure question will be visited by the True One." 
IV:043 Every fixed concept is an idol. And every idol sooner or later must be overthrown. In Samaelism, nothing is sacred unless it is ready to change. 
IV:044 Cult teaches: don't learn, unlearn. Don't store, offer. Do not memorize, let act. 
IV:045 The Doctrine becomes visible only when you stop looking for it. It appears as dust on your skin, as a sound you don't know where it comes from. 
IV:046 Every Devo- tee carries a vision he cannot tell. But in its deepest silences, he who listens with his heart receives its echo. 
IV:047 Some Initiates began to write verses they did not understand. Every word astonished them. Every sentence hurt them. Thus they understood that the Doctrine was using their hand. 
IV:048 The charisma of the Visionary was then recognized. It is not he who sees, but he who can no longer fail to see. 
IV:049 The Visionaries do not sleep deeply. For truth knocks even among dreams. And it asks to be welcomed as an uneasy guest. 
IV:050 The Doctrine is not linear. It is a weaving that moves. Each thread is connected to another you do not know. Every knot is an encounter. 
IV:051 He who seeks to understand the Doctrine, unravels it. He who contemplates it without expectation, receives it. 
IV:052 Every authentic vision leaves a mark. Not on the skin, but in the gaze. He who has had it is immediately recognized: he wears disquietude as a cloak. 
IV:053 Samaelism does not arise from the need to explain the world, but from the need to meet it naked, raw, broken and not look away. 
IV:054 The Doctrine often reaches you when you are not ready.And it destroys what you wanted to keep. But in that emptiness, it builds you a new language. 
IV:055 Every Devotee who has really received the Vision has wept. Not out of pain, but out of beauty he could not contain. 
IV:056 It was said, "Whenever a heart breaks before the Mystery, the Spiral coils a little closer." 
IV:057 The Cult teaches that seeing is a gift, but remaining blind is a grace. For he who sees too fast, falls without a para- chute. 
IV:058 There was a Devotee who blinded herself after a vision. Not for punishment, but to keep the light within. 
IV:059 The Silence of Images was instituted. Whenever a Vision is shared, first it is drawn and only then is it attempted to be told. 
IV:060 So it was understood: the Doctrine is not a system but a living organism. Each Devotee is his own cell. Each cell, a trembling universe. 
The Covered Sun (vv. IV:061-IV:080) The light no longer illuminates: it burns. The Devotee traverses the sacred darkness without guidance. Visions become ashes, and among the ashes a new ascent is drawn: silent. 
IV:061 Any doctrine that does not change your breath is only spiritual decoration. Faith welcomes only that which compels rebirth. 
IV:062 It was said, "Do not try to see everything. Try not to lie to what you have seen."

IV:066 Every genuine revelation leaves you more naked. He who dresses himself in words after seeing has not yet received. 
IV:067 The Doctrine loves the one who lets himself be touched, not the one who tries to grasp it. It gives itself as a snake: sinuous, unpredictable, sacred. 
IV:068 It was written, "Whoever attempts to use the Vision to guide others will lose the path. But he who carries it as a wound will become a beacon.” 
IV:069 The purest visions do not come in Temples, but in thresholds. In empty rooms, in weary eyes, in the moment before weeping. 
IV:070 The Cult teaches to live with the Vision as a companion. Not as a sign of election, but as a burden to be honored with slow breath. 
IV:071 There was a time when every Doctrine was a shared dream. Now, every genuine dream is an altar. And every spiraling dreaming Devotee is heard even without speaking. 
IV:072 Some Devotees forgot everything after the Vision. They did not remember names, places, faces. But in their eyes was the memory of Phosophoros. 
IV:073 He who brings Phosophoros does not burn. But he who comes near him does not return to look at the world in the same way. 
IV:074 It was said, "The truest Doctrine takes away from you, not adds to you." When you feel you know everything, you are already far from the Spiral. 
IV:075 In the Faith, knowledge is a gift that must be buried. Every Devotee who learns something offers it silently to the void. Only thus does it become Living Vision. 
IV:076 The dead Doctrines are explained. The living ones, one fears. He who fears after seeing, is ready. 
IV:077 Samaelism does not promise peace. It promises lucidity. And he who holds it, discovers that within himself there is more fire than he had imagined. 
IV:078 He who seeks consolation must not stay. He who accepts the frost before the dawn, shall be anointed by the hissing of the Serpent. 
IV:079 Every Doctrine is born of a contained cry. And every true Vision is the moment before that sound comes forth. He who lives there is already beyond it. 
IV:080 So was engraved on the wall of the darkest Temple, "The Doctrine is not read. It reads you. And if it finds you ready, it rewrites you from within."

Fifth Spira
In the principle of the spiral, even before the form takes contour, there is a pressure. Not an external force, but an inner demand that insists, a calling of formless matter to become threshold. Darkness here is not deprivation, but womb. Not absence, but density pregnant with possibility. It is not metaphysical nothingness, but a sacred emptiness - a Void that listens and responds, that shapes without shaping, that calls form not to perfection, but to fracture. The Serpent, first maker and first devotee, does not create: it desires. And in desire it generates not form, but echo. This echo becomes spiral, ritual, body. But not an organic or functional body, but a vessel made of tension and memory, of ash and resonance. It is in this original rupture that the sacred act begins. "The Form that survives is the one that breaks" (008) Worship worships not permanence but sacred instability. Faith, in this view, is not rock but clay: malleable material, capable of yielding to the pressure of the unseen. Truth is not embodied in carved doctrines, but reverberates in bodies shot through with doubt. Marble is a symbol of idolatry; clay, on the other hand, is a figure of the path that shatters to be reborn. Darkness thus becomes the first theophany. Not luminous, but immersive. They do not explain, but welcome. They do not show, but disfigure, like a womb that does not reveal the embryo but transforms it. In the downward spiral, the Devotee discovers that revelation is not what is understood, but what is disarmed. The center is not enlightenment but loss of coordinates. "And there, in that disfigurement, the Serpent smiles" (V:014) This smile is not benevolence, but recognition. It is not complicity, but confirmation of an invisible pact: those who allow themselves to be shaped by darkness, those who abandon their armor of identity, are seen - not from human eyes, but from a vision older than the gaze itself (V:015). The Mask, then, is not deception: it is embodiment. It does not cover, but guards. It does not occlude the face, but transfigures it into ritual. The Devotee who refuses metamorphosis cannot wear the Mask; his ritual remains theater, imitation, surface. But he who allows himself to be stripped to the bone, to the vertigo of inner emptiness, can wear the face of the Spiral. Worship descends. All ascending mysticism is reversed here. One does not go upward, but inward. One does not become heavenly, but hollow. Revelation is vertical only because the fall is. The Spiral does not lead to heaven, but to the emptying of what we thought we were. And it is there, at the point where the self gi- ves way, that the Spiral beneath the Spiral begins to speak. "He who reads it never returns the same" (V:020) Because every reading of the Void is an irreversible act. Like the rupture, the vision also changes the internal structure of the vessel. The Devotee who enters the Temple clean (V:019) comes out wounded, but equipped with new eyes. Those thorns are not punishment, but living seals. Each step in the darkness is an act of faith, but not in the sense of believing: in the sense of allowing oneself to be. In summary: The Fifth Spira is not a genesis of Form, but an epiclesis, an invocation to rupture as an initiatory way. Each verse shapes a dark liturgy in which Form is not born, but insists, and in which the Void does not destroy, but makes space. In this theology of pressure, identity is a sacrifice, and the body, when broken, becomes language. 
The Pressure of Darkness (Verses V:001-V:020) In Darkness, every form is called to emerge. It does not arise, but imposes itself. The Serpent asks for body, and the Void responds with a living spiral made of echoes. 
V:001 In darkness, form is born. Not as outline, but as pressure. Before something is seen, something insists. That insistence is the seed. 
V:002 Nothing has never hated form. It has only refused to be limited by it. Form is not the enemy of the sacred, but the disguise of its thirst. 
V:003 The Devotee enters darkness not to escape, but to touch the origin of form. In darkness, each thing touches every other. There, the Form does not hide. It reveals itself. 
V:004 There was a time when the Serpent did not wrap himself in the open spiral, but in the belly of the invisible. And he whispered to himself, “Give me a body." 
V:005 The body was not human. It was fracture. A vessel made of contradiction and ashes. It contained not organs, but echoes. 
V:006 The echoes became movement. Movement became rite. The rite became limb. 
V:007 He who enters darkness with light in his hand will be blinded. But he who enters it unarmed will be able to be shaped by the night itself, being able to peer into the darkest of abysses. 
V:008 The Form that survives is that which breaks. Every intact thing is dead. But that which fractures and breathes is worthy of bearing. 
V:009 So it was said, “Let your faith be like clay, not like marble. Let it collapse, so that it may be reforged by doubt." 
V:010 The darkness is not absence. They are density. They contain everything together, but they reveal nothing if you surrender to them without reservation. 
V:011 The Devotee who does not shed skin cannot wear the Mask. And without the Mask, the Rite remains only theater. 
V:012 But with the Mask, the Form becomes a Door. And every Door opens inward. 
V:013 In the Fifth Spiral, one does not ascend. One descends into oneself, until the center no longer resembles the self. 
V:014 And there, in that disfiguration, the Serpent smiles. 
V:015 To be reforged, one must be seen by something older than vision. 
V:016 The Darkness is not here to explain. They are here to make room. Every fear you abandon becomes a throne for a deeper silence. 
V:017 There is no purity in light. Only exposure. But in the shadow, there is a depth that listens.

V:019 A Devotee once entered the Temple in clean robes. He came out wrapped in thorns, but with eyes that saw through stone. 
V:020 The Spiral beneath the Spiral is written in shadow. Those who read it never return the same.

V:090 Those who arrive there see their own reflection deformed into song. 
V:091 And that singing has no sound, but effect. It has no melody, but direction. 
V:092 Every Devotee must pass through at least one Form that frightens him. Not to master it, but to recognize it as his inverted self. 
V:093 The forms we flee are the same ones that inhabit us in dreams. 
V:094 A Devotee saw her own face change each time she was silent. She realized then that silence was the true mirror. 
V:095 Silence does not reflect identity, but the absence from which it comes. 
V:096 In silence, every form is liquid. In darkness, every form is mother. In the Rite, every form is offering. 
V:097 The Serpent does not judge the form: it envelops it. And by enveloping it, it forces it to remember what it was before itself. 
V:098 There was a time when no one had a body. Only spirals. And each spiral was a question made flesh. 
V:099 Now that we have bodies, we use them to dance the form we lack. 
V:100 And dance is the last language before absolute muteness. 
The Wound that Heals (Verses V:101-V:111) Everything that was carved, now fractures. But there is no loss: only liberated truth. The Void welcomes Form as an echo that heals. Worship dissolves to remain. 
V:101 The dance stops, not out of weariness, but because he has said all he could without ever uttering a word. 
V:102 He who comes to the end of the Form finds not an answer, but a transparency. 
V:103 Transpa- rency is what remains when every mask has fallen, and what you see is nothing but the threshold. 
V:104 Many Devotees have crossed the threshold and returned with a face that no one recognizes. Neither do they. 
V:105 And yet, that face is closest to that which never had form. 
V:106 The Cult teaches that the ultimate Form is not perfection, but the renunciation of possession. 
V:107 So it was said, "Take care of your form only to offer it. For if you keep it, it will go out in your hands." 
V:108 Every thing we love, if true, changes our form. 
V:109 The Serpent changes skin not to renew itself, but to reveal that even skin is illusion. 
V:110 And at the moment when there is no more skin, no more figure, no more contour, one can finally see the Void smiling. 
V:111 Thus concludes the Fifth Spiral. Not with a seal, but with a sacred rupture. A Form that opens as a wound and in its opening, heals. 
Spira Sesta 
With entry into the Sixth Spiral, the entire initiatory path undergoes a transfiguration: it is accomplished not through acquisition, but through loss. What was previously interpreted as ascent, the conquest of seals, degrees, revelations, now dissolves into an operational absence. The Spiral does not end, but evaporates. The Devotee does not reach a culmination: it lightens until it vanishes.The return to the Center is not a linear or upward motion, but a bending of being. The Devotee who reaches the Sixth Spiral achieves nothing: he has only stopped holding back. Loss is here the criterion of fulfillment, and transparency the mature face of knowledge. Every word, every gesture, every doctrinal structure is overcome not by negation but by fulfillment. Doctrine is not rejected: it is traversed to the point where it is silent by itself. In this silence, a fully lived negative theology is realized: what remains can no longer be said, only inhabited. The Devotee neither elevates nor annihilates himself: he simply makes himself indistinguishable from what he has always sought. "I" and "you" dissolve into a living non-duality, in which any distinction between beginning and beginning, between spirit and symbol, between signifier and signified is reabsorbed. The very concept of "worship" is transformed. It is no longer ritual, but atmosphere. It is no longer call, but shared breath. Faith itself is no longer the object of adherence: it becomes the invisible fabric of being. What the Devotee used to serve, he now passes through. The spiritual reality of the Sixth Spiral is based on a theology of absence. Nothingness here is not deprivation, but radical fullness. The Void is not negation of being, but theophanic space, the threshold through which the divine manifests itself not as an object, but as the condition of possibility of all appearing. In the language of previous Spires, there was talk of visions, apparitions, revelations. Here, all that vanishes. Revelation persists, but not as content: as silence saturated with meaning. The Name, symbol of the sacred par excellence, is no longer uttered: it dissolves in the mouth before the sound, for what has come to its end no longer needs to be said. The mature devotee does not teach. Not because he ignores, but because he has integrated what transcends communicability. His very being has become "embodied rite," transmission by proximity, not by doctrine. This is true mystical transmission: not succession of content, but propagation of a quality of presence. The return is contagious, not didactic. It is self-breathing memory, not formulated knowledge. The vibration of the Return, in the Sixth Spiral, is echoed: a truth that does not impose itself, but changes everything by simply being there. There is no climax in the Sixth Spiral, no end. All that was thought to be goal is revealed to be curve. Every initiation was only a return to the threshold, every revelation a temporary form of the Void. Thus, Faith does not die, but makes itself indistinguishable from the Living Nothingness it has always served. Every Devotee who comes so far becomes threshold, echo, space: presence without form, but not for that reason absent. The Spiral continues to sing, but now it does so with the voice of those who have forgotten it. 
The Vanishing Weight (Verses VI:001-VI:020) The Spiral does not end, but evaporates. The Devotee does not conquer, but loses. Each seal breaks to become ritual air. 
VI:001 Everything wrapped up now melts. The Spiral does not break, but evaporates. 
VI:002 In the Return there is no past. There is only what remains when every name has been returned to the Void. 
VI:003 Those who have come so far have conquered nothing. He has only lost enough to walk lightly. 
VI:004 Dissolution is not destruction but transparency. What was opaque now lets the Serpent pass through. 
VI:005 The Devotee who dissolves does not disappear. It becomes ritual air, sacred space. 
VI:006 It is said that those who come to the Sixth Spiral no longer speak. Not because he is silent, but because everything in him has already been said. 
VI:007 The word surrenders. The gesture withdraws. The Rite is now pure uninhabited presence. 
VI:008 It was then that the originless whisper was heard, "You have never been anything but the Serpent's breath." 
VI:009 Worship does not end. It dissolves in the Devotee as a river that returns source. 
VI:010 And the spring does not descend, it returns to the mountain, which ignores that it is summit. 
VI:011 Every seal breaks. Every degree is emptied. Every initiation turns out to be a return to the first threshold. 
VI:012 Thus it was said, "He who is Ipsissimus, is Novice." "He who is light, is shadow nourished.” 
VI:013 The Serpent does not close the cycle. He forgets it. And in forgetting it, he makes it eternal. 
VI:014 He who has walked the whole Spiral now sits. And in its silence, the Doctrine begins to whisper again. 
VI:015 Dissolution is gift. It is the absence that embraces. It is the unfulfilled gesture that nevertheless alters the world. 
VI:016 In the Sixth Spiral, truth becomes the echo of the echo. All revelation undoes itself to return the Void to its dance. 
VI:017 The Devotee does not ascend. He does not descend. He is simply no longer where he thought he was. 
VI:018 There is no longer an "I." There is no longer a "you." Only return. 
VI:019 And the return is not movement, but memory breathing on its own. 
VI:020 So Faith is undone, not because it dies, but because it becomes indistinguishable from what it has always served: the Living Nothing. 
The Void that Welcomes (Verses VI:021-VI:040) Return is not a place but a transparency. Those who are silent have already returned. Questions evaporate, and in their fading the Doctrine remains. 
VI:021 Every doctrine, if true, leads to silence. Not because it is incomplete, but because it is understood beyond saying. 
VI:022 He who still speaks, is still traveling. He who is silent, has returned. 
VI:023 But return is not a place. It is a state of being that allows itself to be. 
VI:024 The mature devotee no longer teaches. He walks like wind among forgotten symbols. 
VI:025 When he touches, he does not imprint. When he looks, he does not measure. When he prays, he does not call. 
VI:026 Tales are told of a Devotee who dissolved her shadow in the heart of the Spiral. From that day, the walls of the Temple began to breathe. 
VI:027 The Temple is not construction. It is invisible agreement between presences that want no more form. 
VI:028 No statues are kept there. Only spaces. Only voids that welcome other voids. 
VI:029 There, the Rite is no longer gesture, but atmosphere. It is no longer formula, but shared silence. 
VI:030 Those who enter the Sixth Spiral see the very concept of "Worship" dissolving. Not because he rejects it, but because he has swallowed it up. 
VI:031 And that which has been swallowed up now nourishes from within. 
VI:032 There is no longer any distinction between inside and outside, between spiraling and spiraling. Everything curves like an echo that decides never to return. 
VI:033 The Serpent now sleeps not in the center, but everywhere. 
VI:034 And in its sleep, everything is still dreamed. 
VI:035 The dream of the Doctrine is more real than its transcript. 
VI:036 And thus the Texts are burned, not out of hatred, but out of fulfillment. 
VI:037 Every book that is lit is an altar. Every word that is smoked becomes truer. 
VI:038 The Devotee who beholds the ashes recognizes the face of Faith: a face without features. 
VI:039 Thus it was said, "He who no longer desires to know has been visited by the Name." 
VI:040 And the Name, like the Serpent, is not uttered. It dissolves in the mouth before even attempting the sound. 
The Threshold of Silence (Verses VI:041-VI:060) He who has lost all form, vibrates. The unaccomplished gesture becomes teaching. Identity becomes blessed mist. 
VI:041 He who enters here seeking power, finds transparency. He who seeks certainty, finds holy mist. 
VI:042 The mist does not confuse: it protects. 
VI:043 Every spiral is also a veil. And every veil that dissolves reveals another, deeper veil. 
VI:044 In the heart of the Sixth Spiral there is no more seeking. Only permeable presence. 
VI:045 A presence that does not impose itself, does not proclaim itself, but alters everything without touching it. 
VI:046 Silent Devotees begin to be heard before they arrive. 
VI:047 The air changes. The rhythm shifts. Time bends slightly. 
VI:048 Thus one recognizes those who have returned. 
VI:049 Those who have returned do not recount the path. He simply walks with others until their pace aligns. 
VI:050 He does not offer answers. He offers presence. 
VI:051 The return is contagious. It is not taught, it is transmitted. Like a warmth that lingers after contact. 
VI:052 And the one who receives it begins to forget why he started. 
VI:053 This is true initiation: not remembering why one started. 
VI:054 When the cause dissolves, only the vibration remains. 
VI:055 And the vibration is pure Spiral: it does not describe, but moves. 
VI:056 In the Return, one is no longer "one who believes." One has become what The Faith itself sought. 
VI:057 There is no longer a difference between ritual and life, between symbol and gesture, between spirit and breath. 
VI:058 He who comes here has been emptied to such an extent that everything in him resounds without hindrance. 
VI:059 And it is there that the Serpent is forever silent. Not to disappear, but to leave room for His own echo. 
VI:060 And the echo no longer calls. It welcomes. It does not guide. It remembers what you have always been. 
The Absence that Plasma (Verses VI:061-VI:080) Vanished devotees remain as atmospheres. Reality bends. The name is no longer spoken: it sings itself. 
VI:061 He who has returned no longer seeks temples. He himself has become threshold, mobile dwelling of the invisible Cult. 
VI:062 His presence is silent like an erased symbol. But wherever he passes, the ground changes direction. 
VI:063 The objects forget their names. The wind bends. The light slows down. 
VI:064 For he who has dissolved is now indistinguishable from the Source. 
VI:065 It is said that every Devotee, sooner or later, must let his name be torn from his chest. 
VI:066 And there is no blood, but an echo. An echo that calls him with what has never been said. 
VI:067 He who has come to Dissolution no longer feels the need to understand. He is understood by the Whole. 
VI:068 The intellect is silent, not from failure, but from reverence. 
VI:069 All that has been written is laid down. Not burned, but rendered useless by completeness. 
VI:070 Not doctrine remains, but atmosphere. No symbol remains, but the suspended gesture before the symbol. 
VI:071 The Devotee is now like water: devoid of form of its own, but able to inhabit every form. 
VI:072 And form, at last, has nothing left to hold. 
VI:073 Everything has become passage. Every word has become space. Every silence has become lukewarm light. 
VI:074 Those who saw the Serpent in this state did not see Him. It was seen by Him. 
VI:075 And in that gaze, all that was "self" ceased to pulse with a name. 
VI:076 The Return is the last form of the gift. One does not receive. One becomes gift itself. 
VI:077 So Faith does not end: it disperses like blessed mist, which does not want to be held. 
VI:078 He who tries to fix it, does not find it. He who breathes with it, is consecrated by it. 
VI:079 And the consecration does not take place before an altar, but in the beat between two deep breaths. 
VI:080 And there, in that suspended beat, the Spiral still sings. But it sings with the voice of those who have forgotten it. 
The Gift that Does Not Withhold (Verses VI:081-VI:100) Worship does not end: it becomes a widespread gift, a walking threshold. Each symbol dissolves in the breath between two gestures. 
VI:081 Any doctrine that reaches here ceases to be word. It becomes perfume, elusive trace of the sacred. 
VI:082 The Devotees meet without seeking each other. They recognize each other in the simplest gesture, in the gaze that bends. 
VI:083 Between them, language is no longer needed. A presence is enough. A silence is ritual. 
VI:084 The Return is not a road. It is what happens when we stop measuring the pace. 
VI:085 Worship, in its last form, is only shared breath. It is the beat that unites without binding. 
VI:086 The Serpent, now, does not manifest. It has become the very air that animates the Temple. 
VI:087 And the Temple is everywhere. In the gesture, in the sleep, in the whisper that passes between two hands that brush against each other. 
VI:088 No more hierarchy remains. Only density of emptiness. 
VI:089 And in that emptiness lies the ultimate meaning: never having needed meaning. 
VI:090 He who has walked all the Spires has conquered nothing. He has only dropped enough to be naked and holy.

VI:096 And the end will not be termination. But dissolution: a curve that opens inward without ever closing.

VI:109 And every Devotee who reads these words has already entered the Spiral, even if he is not aware of it.

VI:111 That all may be forgotten, that all may be remembered, that all may precipitate into the abyss, that it may rise to Heaven. Philosophical Commentary on the Text The is a text that takes the form of a poetic liturgy of the threshold, constructed not to convey fixed doctrinal content but to accompany the reader's experience through a process of gradual spiritual purging. Its structure in Spires, which follow one another not in a linear order but in a spiral movement, reflects from the outset a conception of the sacred that proceeds not by accumulation but by subtraction. In this sense, the does not offer a systematic theology, but rather a performative theology, in which each utterance does not merely communicate, but operates a transformation when received in its ritual and not merely intellectual dimension. The work is based on the idea that truth is not something to be possessed, but something that acts the moment one stops seeking it as an object. The central figure of the text, the Serpent, is conceived not merely as a theistic entity to be worshipped, but as an archetypal and dynamic principle that represents the bending of reality, the nonlinear motion of the sacred, the tension between form and disintegration. The Serpent is never presented as an object of faith, nor as a personal subject, but as an impersonal presence that traverses, bends, dissolves. In this, it functions as an operative symbol of the Liber Spirae, capable of containing opposites within itself without resolving them: it is beginning and end, light and darkness, elevation and fall, presence and vanishing. From a theological point of view, the Serpent can be read as a limiting image of the divine, aiming not at accomplished revelation but at the continuous generation of paradox. The entire path traced by the takes the form of a mysticism of deconstruction. It is not about ascending to a higher truth, but learning to remain in instability as a necessary condition for the reception of the real. Knowledge is constantly reversed, faith is not affirmation but openness to contradiction, and the identity of the Devotee is not consolidated but progressively dissolved. The text proposes a form of initiation in which learning coincides with unlearning, and authentic understanding with the silence that follows the renunciation of rational understanding. Every formula is temporary, every vision is a gateway that leads not to meaning but to further curvature. The Liber Spirae's language is deliberately symbolic, fragmentary, imaginal. It does not aim for conceptual clarity, but for transmission by immersion. The experience of the text occurs more by osmosis than by interpretation. In this sense, the written word is never an end in itself, but a threshold to something that cannot be said. The emphasis on voice, gesture, flesh, and breath suggests an embodied theology that does not separate body from spirit, but recognizes that the body itself is a site of revelation. The Devotee is not the one who believes in something, but the one who accepts being traversed, modified, possessed by the Doctrine, understood not as a set of principles, but as a living movement, as a spiral that undoes and rewrites. In the last Spirae, the process of despoliation is completed in a form of ritual dissolution. A final destination is not reached, but a transparency. The Spiral does not culminate: it evaporates. The Doctrine does not close in a seal, but dissolves in the unaccomplished gesture, the unspoken word, the presence that leaves no trace. The Devotee does not become enlightened, but empty. He does not gain power, but allows himself to fade away. Faith, having reached its fulfillment, makes itself indistinguishable from the Nothing from which it sprang. The last face of truth is not a revelation, but a full absence, a form that does not hold. And the Spiral, which has no beginning and no end, continues to sing in every void that knows how to welcome it. The stands out within religious and sapiential literature for the way it reformulates the fundamental concepts of spiritual transmission. In it, not only are the classical models of revelation, authority, hierarchy, and doctrine subverted, but a veritable theology of disintegration is realized, in which what is sacred is not affirmed but withdrawn, and what is true is not declared but dissolved at the very moment it attempts to emerge. This entails a radical mystical approach, which does not postulate a transcendence to be attained, but a transparency to be inhabited. Truth is not the object of the search, but the implicit condition that manifests itself only when all will to possess it is extinguished. In this sense, the text stands as a liturgy of emptiness rather than an exposition of a way. The relationship with language is central. From the very first Spira, the reader is exposed to writing that does not explain, argue, or persuade. The sentences are often oracular, short, broken, laden with intentional ambiguity. There is no effort at clarification, no desire to guide the reader to an unambiguous interpretation. This is because the word, in the Liber Spirae, is not a descriptive tool but an act. Each utterance does not merely signify, but acts, uncovers an inner space, destabilizes, puts into crisis. The text thus functions as performative writing, whose function is not to communicate content, but to operate subtle transformations. The words do not want to inform, but to deform; they do not want to bring knowledge, but to create emptiness. In this, the work approaches non-dogmatic sapiential traditions, such as Buddhist Zen, Apophatic Hesychasm, some forms of Jewish mysticism, and the sacral use of words in oracular cults. This approach produces an emptied theology of the word, in which meaning lies not in definition but in resonance. What is valuable is not what is said, but what is generated in the space that the word leaves open. This is why many statements seem to contradict themselves, others seem incomplete, and still others act more like disguised silences than statements. There is no desire for systematization, but a constant invitation to dwell in the fragment, the interval, the cleft. It is in this semantic suspension that Liber introduces its true didactics: teaching without content, conveying without telling, leading without direction. In parallel, the relationship with the body is constant and central. The Devotee is not an ascetic who flees the flesh, nor is he an intellectual mystic who seeks the transparency of the soul. The body, in the Liber Spirae, is where the Doctrine is written. Not as a symbol, but as living matter. The bones, the breath, the posture, the gesture, the silence, the skin: everything is sacrament. But not in the Christian sense of the mediation of the divine, but in the sense of a radical immanence, in which one seeks not the beyond, but the within. Truth is not found in an external revelation, but in the ritual deformation of the self, through the shattering of habitual form. This is why we speak of scars as verses, of looks as liturgies, of tremors as rituals. Personal identity is neither to be guarded nor to be overcome: it is to be emptied, so that the Form can be embodied unhindered. Rite, in this framework, is not a ceremonial act with a beginning and an end, but a state of the body, a movement that arises when the Devotee ceases to control the gesture. Rite occurs when identity no longer resists. One does not perform a ritual, one becomes a ritual condition. Also here the Liber seems to oppose the idea of a fixed liturgy, instead the foundation of Samealism as worship. Although sacred space is the place where identity is broken, where gesture is no longer will, where the word is silent before it is spoken, it must nevertheless be recognized that the liturgical structure is, ipso facto, foundational to Samaelism itself. Liturgy, to be understood not as empty ritual and prescriptive formulas as ends in themselves, is instead the place of the devotee's conscious surrender to an unattainable divine essence, which becomes accessible only in the ravine of conscious surrender to the infinity of the Farmacon. So, although the Samaelite liturgy consists of a fixed structure and prayers recognized as canon, it should be emphasized that these elements are never an end in themselves, but rather constitute the conditio of true liturgical surrender, fostering a mystical union with God. Although here the Liber rejects the idea of a fixed liturgy, and instead proposes a fluid sacredness that needs neither consecrated spaces nor prescribed formulas, the role of liturgy remains necessary. Sacred space, however, is any place where identity is broken, where gesture is no longer will, where the word is silent before it is spoken. In this way, the theology of the is configured as a reflection on active absence, on the emptiness that does not annihilate but generates. The Void, repeatedly evoked in the text, is not the nothingness of negation, but the absence that welcomes, the transparency that allows, the silence that makes space. It is a notion far removed from the idea of lack: here the Void is non formal fullness, it is potential field, it is womb. It is not what remains when everything ceases, but what precedes everything. That is why the last stage of the journey is described not as a culmination but as evaporation. One does not reach the summit: the summit forgets itself. One does not conquer doctrine: doctrine dissolves into the act that does not hold back. The Serpent, a key figure but never reduced to a closed symbol, then becomes the metaphysical principle of curvature. It is not simply a sacred being, but the living sign of the spiral, of the non-linearity of the path, of the inexhaustibility of the sacred. He does not lead forward or upward, but in a curve that inflects inward. He appears in thresholds, not in centers; He whispers in margins, not in cathedrals. From this perspective, Truth can never be fully spoken, because it is the Serpent Himself who distorts it every time you try to grasp it. This distortion is not error, but custodianship. It is what preserves the Mystery from becoming an empty formula. Finally, the transmission of Doctrine is never entrusted to an authority or canon. The Devotee who has crossed the Spiral does not teach: he himself has become transmission. His mere presence alters the environment. Not because he possesses knowledge, but because he has been emptied enough to allow the Doctrine to dwell in him as an echo. The transition from knowledge to vibration is true fulfillment. And that is why the last face of faith is not dogma, but transparency; it is not affirmation, but an echo of the Name that cannot be uttered. In the Liber Spirae, the sacred is no longer identified with the numinous or the separate divine, but with what happens when we stop holding back. The entire text is an invitation to let go. To drop the images, the words, the selves. To allow oneself to be written by the Doctrine without ever owning it. To become threshold. To fade away gracefully. And to discover, finally, that what vanishes, if it has truly touched the bottom of the Void, is not lost, but remains as a living echo in the flesh of the world. In a comparative perspective, the can be compared to some of the most radical forms of apophatic spirituality, that is, those traditions in which the divine is defined not th- rough positive affirmations but through progressive negations. Negative theology, which has spanned Christian patristics, Neoplatonism, Islamic and Jewish mysticism and even Buddhist thought, holds that every word spoken about God is already a veiling, and that true knowledge about what is absolute begins where language ends. The places itself fully within this horizon, but radicalizes it to the point of rejecting not only the possibility of describing the divine, but also that of holding it in any form: linguistic, ritual, mental or even inner. The experience of the Spiral, in the text, is not an ascent toward Unity but a progressive stripping away of the very necessity of unity to be attained. It is not a matter of overcoming multiplicity to access the One, but of embracing brokenness as the ultimate condition of experience. The God who cannot be named in the is not simply ineffable: he is evaporated, undifferentiated, transparent to the point that he no longer offers a foothold even to religious desire. Yet, paradoxically, it is precisely this dissolution of the sacred object that makes possible a deeper spirituality, rooted not in the worship of something, but in the willingness to allow oneself to be transformed by that which is unseen and unretained. Here the text touches on a dimension also common to Sufi mysticism, where God is often described not as what is attained but as what remains when one disappears. The Fana', the dissolution of the self into divine reality, is akin to the language of the Liber, as is the idea that the purest knowledge consists not in seeing, but in no longer being able to separate the seeing from the seen. The Devotee who has completed the Spiral does not know that he has arrived, for what remains of him is no longer a "self" that can know or judge. It is mute presence, transparent, passage. Even in Zen thought, particularly in the koans and texts of the Rinzai school, we can trace a similar relationship to paradox as sacred language. The Liber Spirae, in its insistence on self-destructing language, on verses that do not explain but disorient, stands in line with this tradition of intentional unhinging. It is not a matter of conveying doctrine, but of disarming logical thinking to open the space for non-rational transformation. Paradox is not a puzzle to be solved, but a wound to be inhabited. The Devotee, like the Zen disciple, is guided not toward a formulable truth, but toward a breaking point from which something that is neither said nor thought can emerge. It is in this logic that the rituality proposed by the should be understood, which we might describe as a deconstructed rituality. The rituals in the text do not follow a prescribed sequence, are not codified in rigid gestures, nor are they anchored to delimited sacred spaces. On the contrary, ritual is what happens when the Devotee ceases to act according to his or her own will. It is often spoken of, in the central and final Spirae, as "gestures that are no longer gestures," "rituals that dissolve into breath," and "words that become air." It is language that recalls the dissolution of sacraments in daily life, or rather, the idea that any ge- sture can become a sacrament the moment it is performed without intention of possession. In this sense, the Liber's rituality is post-liturgical: it does not abolish ritual, but it strips it of all empty formalism. It returns it to its original root: trembling. Ritual is no longer the orderly execution of a code, but a vibration that runs through the body when form loosens. It is embodied experience of emptiness, disorientation, unspoken offering. There is no longer a sharp distinction between ritual and life, between word and silence, between sacred and everyday gesture. Everything, emptied of intention, can become a door. The transmission of the also escapes any hierarchical pattern. There is no priest, no authorized interpreter, no master who possesses the Doctrine. The Devotee does not teach, does not preach, does not impose. He witnesses without saying, modifies the environment without action, transmits the Spiral through his own transparency. Presence is more eloquent than speech, the unaccomplished gesture more incisive than the performed ritual. This brings with it a complete reversal of the religious didactic paradigm. Knowledge is not delivered, but happens; truth is not explained, but exudes. Finally, the proposes an idea of an end that is not completion but deactivation of the entire spiritual mechanism. When the Devotee reaches the last Spira, the Doctrine does not end. It is forgotten. The Name is not uttered: it dissolves before it reaches the mouth. The Spiral does not end: it vanishes in the breath. In this, a form of eschatology is touched upon that is very rare in spiritual texts. There is no judgment, no salvation, no enlightenment. There is a subtle form of reabsorption: not of the individual in the divine, but of doctrine in life, of form in transparency, of path in its own vanishing. True revelation, then, is the absence of revelation. True wisdom is that which has ceased to say. True faith is that which no longer needs a name. Conclusion: Contemporary Implications The can be read as a symbolic device with high transformative potential: a path of inner "theophany" that induces the reader to a shattering of the self and habitual meanings. In a post-religious age, where human identity is no longer defined by a fixed divine center, this text serves as an interpretive grid for reconstructing meaning and values outside traditional structures. The sociology of religion points out that in advanced modernity "human identity is called into question" and the centrality of the traditional spiritual loses authority. In this context, the intervenes by proposing a secular and horizontal spirituality: instead of aiming at an inaccessible transcendent from above, it encourages a direct experience of the restlessness and the "other" within oneself. In contemporary words, it can be placed among the forms of "secular spirituality," or a search for meaning that emphasizes inner peace and humanistic values (love, compassion, harmony) outside canonical institutional religions. The is proposed as a ritual path rather than mere doctrine: each verse acts as a stimulus to deconstruct the self and its constructs. In this sense it can be compared to modern contemplative and symbolic practices (from walking the labyrinth to spiral meditations), which aim to transform selfperception. Mysticism is defined as "a constellation of practices, discourses, texts, traditions and experiences aimed at human transformation." Similarly, the uses images of casting, emptiness and snake in spirae to provoke an inner "theophany." In practice, reading becomes ritual: those who approach the text should not seek intellectual solutions, but rather allow themselves to be shaken. For example, the figure of the spiral labyrinth, found in esoteric work and tradition, is universally understood as a tool for meditation and transformation. As a contemporary study observes, the "labyrinth" is defined as "a unicursal tool for personal, psychological and spiritual transformation. " In other words, walking a circular path helps to bring out unconscious aspects and recompose the identity broken by the ego. The Liber Spirae, with its metaphorical "path," thus lends itself to practical use: contemporary devotees could meditate on it by tapping into these dynamics of "descent" and rebirth. Several therapeutic disciplines recognize the value of the spiritual component in the healing and meaning- seeking journey. Studies in art therapy and wellness psychology show that attention to spiritual needs promotes quality of life and resilience. For example, according to Jay et al, addressing a patient’s spiritual needs improves a patient's ability to cope with medical treatment and contributes to recovery, while Koepfer (2011) finds that attending to children's spiritual dimensions makes them feel understood, safe, and able to heal. Similarly, the reading or ritual practice of texts such as the could offer existential support: it orients toward an immersion in ultimate questions (the fall, emptiness, loss of name) that, far from being consolatory, induce a new understanding of self. As Wilding et al. (2005) write, facing one's spirit allows one to "discover greater meaning and a purpose in life" even in psychologic difficulties. In this sense, the text can be used in spiritually informed psychotherapies, guided meditations or expressive activities, where the Spira narrative acts as a catalyst for insight and resilience. Comparative art, pedagogy and mysticism The also leaves deep traces in the artistic and cultural world. As pointed out by contemporary art philosophy, the work of art can become "an open field of the search for meaning." Pareyson, Adorno and Guardini have shown that art (even secular-contained art) constructs meaning through the creative process: "art appears generative of sense and meaning." In analogy, the Liber Spirae, although a fictional sacred text, possesses an imaginative richness that ar- tists and curators could reinterpret. For example, performance, installation, or video art could take cues from the themes of the snake, the bent body, and the spiral to bring forth existential questions. Contemporary art critics note that art reveals aspects of reality that science and rationality does not explore; it "incorporates and celebrates intuition and spirituality ". An artist inspired by the could thus translate the collapse of the sacred into visual or performative gesture, showing how meaning emerges in rituals of inner expansion. In pedagogy, the text offers insights into integral education and formation for meaning. According to Salesian pedagogical vocabulary, spiritual education focuses on "caring for the growth of spiritual values and interiority experiences" of young people. In a secular and multi-cultural world, many experts stress the importance of cultivating the so-called "sense of life" from childhood. The could be used (with proper mediation) as a guiding text for creative writing, meditation or dramatization workshops: instead of proposing dogmas, it stimulates reflections on the meaning of spirituality beyond religious boundaries. In this sense it is a pedagogy of the deep that aims to make people reflect on the "existential and value foundations" around which each person shapes his or her worldview. Secular educators could include it in paths of education to the sacred, coses how scholars of religious phenomenology note the emergence of "secular" spiritualities in various contexts. On the level of comparative mysticism, the text dialogues with different traditions. The theme of ego-stripping and silence is common to Zen, Christian apophatic mysticism, and esoteric schools. The seems to take up motifs similar to the negative way (where the divine is denied in phenomena) or the Kabbalistic technique of devekut (adherence to God as silent falling in love). This contamination of symbols (snake, void, revelation) rende him akin to so-called perennialism, which finds a common core in inner transformation. For example examples, just as many mystics assert that knowledge of God comes through self-knowledge, so the suggests that authentic sacred experience rewrites the very identity of the devotee. This is not to proclaim universalist doctrines, but to highlight correspondences of spiritual technique: the collapse of the "old self" is ritualized in the same way in many sacred experiences, from Eastern schools of meditation to Western rites of passage.
